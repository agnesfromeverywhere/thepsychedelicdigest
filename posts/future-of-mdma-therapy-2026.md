MDMA-assisted therapy is one of the most compelling stories in modern psychiatry. Not because of hype. Because of data. In the most rigorous clinical trials the field has produced, MDMA combined with psychotherapy achieved remission rates for PTSD that no existing treatment has matched. The question now is not whether it works. The question is when it will be available, to whom, and on whose terms.

## What Did the Phase 3 Trials Actually Show?

MAPS completed the first Phase 3 randomised controlled trial of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD in 2023. The results were striking by any clinical standard.

**67% of participants who received MDMA-assisted therapy no longer met diagnostic criteria for PTSD after treatment, compared to 32% in the placebo group.** At a 12-month follow-up, the majority of those who responded were still in remission.

Participants in these trials had severe, chronic PTSD, with an average duration of 14 years and an average of 3.5 prior treatments that had failed. These were not easy cases. The baseline expectation of remission from any treatment was low. MDMA therapy substantially exceeded it.

The treatment involved three MDMA sessions of approximately eight hours each, bookended by preparatory and integration therapy sessions. The total treatment package spanned approximately five months.

## What Is Happening With MAPS-2?

MAPS-2 is the confirmatory Phase 3 trial required for FDA approval. It is currently underway, fully enrolled, with results expected in late 2026 or early 2027. A positive readout would allow MAPS to submit a New Drug Application to the FDA, with approval potentially following by 2028.

The FDA granted MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD Breakthrough Therapy designation in 2017, which accelerates the review process. The question of whether the FDA will ultimately approve it remains open, but the regulatory pathway is clearer than for almost any other psychedelic-based treatment.

## Who Will Benefit Most From MDMA Therapy?

The population with the strongest evidence base is adults with chronic, treatment-resistant PTSD. This includes:

- **Military veterans and first responders** with combat or occupational trauma. The DVA in Australia has already approved case-by-case funding for eligible veterans.
- **Survivors of sexual assault and childhood abuse**, who were well represented in the MAPS trials and showed strong responses.
- **People with complex trauma histories** who have not responded to prolonged exposure, EMDR, or medication.

MDMA's mechanism makes it particularly well suited to trauma treatment. By reducing activity in the amygdala while maintaining prefrontal cortex function, MDMA allows people to revisit traumatic memories without the overwhelming fear response that typically makes trauma processing so difficult. It creates a window of psychological safety that standard therapy cannot reliably produce.

## What Are the Obstacles to Access?

The most significant obstacle is the eight-hour session length. Unlike ketamine, which fits into a one-hour clinic appointment, MDMA therapy requires extended clinical time, two trained therapists per session per MAPS protocol, and specialised facilities. This makes it expensive to deliver at scale.

MAPS estimates that a full course of MDMA-assisted therapy could cost between $15,000 and $20,000 USD in the US. Without insurance coverage, access will be severely limited to those who can pay out of pocket.

In Australia, the TGA approved MDMA as a Schedule 8 medicine for PTSD in 2023, but the same access barriers that limit psilocybin therapy apply: fewer than 100 authorised prescribers nationally, no Medicare coverage, and costs in the $20,000 to $40,000 AUD range. ## What Comes After PTSD?

MDMA is being studied for conditions beyond PTSD, including social anxiety disorder in autistic adults, alcohol use disorder, and eating disorders. The mechanism suggests particular promise for any condition where fear responses and interpersonal disconnection are central features.

The Eli Lilly acquisition of AtaiBeckley, whose pipeline includes oral R-MDMA for social anxiety disorder, signals significant pharmaceutical interest in expanding MDMA's clinical applications. ## FAQ

**When will MDMA therapy be approved by the FDA?**
If MAPS-2 produces positive results in late 2026 or early 2027, FDA approval could follow by 2028. The Breakthrough Therapy designation accelerates the review process.

**Is MDMA therapy approved in Australia?**
MDMA was rescheduled to Schedule 8 in Australia in July 2023, making it legally prescribable by TGA Authorised Prescribers for PTSD. Treatment is available but expensive and limited in geographic reach.

**How many MDMA sessions are needed?**
The MAPS protocol involves three MDMA sessions, each approximately eight hours, along with preparatory and integration therapy sessions. The full treatment spans approximately five months.

**Is MDMA addictive?**
At the doses and frequency used in therapeutic settings, MDMA does not produce physical dependence. The therapeutic protocol involves a small number of widely spaced sessions, which is very different from patterns of recreational use.

**Who should not receive MDMA therapy?**
MDMA is contraindicated for people with uncontrolled hypertension, significant cardiovascular disease, active psychosis, and certain medication combinations including MAOIs. A thorough medical screening is part of the treatment process.
