Ketamine is having a moment. While psilocybin and MDMA dominate the psychedelic therapy headlines, ketamine is already legal, already available, and already changing lives for Australians with treatment-resistant depression. It is not a perfect treatment. But for many people who have exhausted other options, it is the most accessible pathway to relief that currently exists.

## How Does Ketamine Work for Depression?

Ketamine works differently from every antidepressant that came before it. Standard antidepressants target serotonin, noradrenaline, or dopamine systems and take weeks to show effect. Ketamine targets the glutamate system, specifically NMDA receptors, and produces measurable antidepressant effects within hours to days.

**The neurobiological mechanism involves rapid synaptogenesis**, the formation of new synaptic connections in the prefrontal cortex, an area of the brain heavily implicated in mood regulation and executive function. Ketamine essentially creates a window of heightened neuroplasticity during which the brain can form new, more adaptive patterns.

A [2019 meta-analysis](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(18)30476-8/fulltext) published in The Lancet Psychiatry found that a single ketamine infusion produced significant reductions in depression severity within 24 hours in the majority of participants with treatment-resistant depression. **Effects typically persist for one to two weeks from a single infusion**, which is why treatment protocols usually involve a series of sessions.

## What Is the Difference Between Ketamine and Esketamine?

This distinction matters in Australia specifically. Esketamine, sold under the brand name Spravato, is an intranasal formulation of the S-enantiomer of ketamine. **Spravato was listed on the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme in May 2025 for treatment-resistant depression**, making it the first subsidised psychedelic-adjacent treatment in Australia.

This means eligible patients can access esketamine through an authorised prescriber at significantly reduced cost compared to IV ketamine infusions. The PBS listing requires confirmed treatment-resistant depression and initiation under specialist supervision.

IV ketamine infusions remain available through private clinics but are not PBS-listed and cost between $500 and $1,500 AUD per session. A standard treatment course of six infusions over two to three weeks costs between $3,000 and $9,000 AUD out of pocket.

## What Does a Ketamine Treatment Session Feel Like?

Ketamine at therapeutic doses produces a dissociative experience. This is different from a full psilocybin journey but shares some features: altered perception of time and space, visual effects, a sense of distance from one's ordinary thought patterns, and often a feeling of profound calm or perspective shift.

The experience typically lasts 40 to 60 minutes for an IV infusion, or 30 to 40 minutes for intranasal esketamine. Medical staff are present throughout. Most people find it manageable and some find it genuinely therapeutic in the moment, though the acute experience is not considered the primary mechanism of action.

Post-session, most people rest for an hour before being cleared to leave. You cannot drive after a ketamine session and should have someone collect you.

## Who Is Eligible for Ketamine Therapy in Australia?

IV ketamine through private clinics is accessible to most adults with a depression diagnosis through referral from a GP or psychiatrist. There are contraindications including uncontrolled hypertension, active psychosis, and certain cardiovascular conditions.

PBS-subsidised Spravato requires confirmed treatment-resistant depression, defined as failure to respond to at least two adequate antidepressant trials, and must be initiated by a psychiatrist.

## Where Can You Access Ketamine Therapy in Australia?

Ketamine clinics are operating in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, and Adelaide. GoodMind Therapeutics offers Spravato alongside clinician-guided psilocybin and MDMA programs. Several specialist psychiatry practices now offer ketamine infusions with integration support.

Ask your GP for a referral to a psychiatrist with experience in ketamine therapy, or contact ketamine clinics directly as they can advise on whether a GP referral is required.

## FAQ

**Is ketamine the same as a psychedelic?**
Ketamine is classified as a dissociative anaesthetic rather than a classical psychedelic. It does not primarily target serotonin 2A receptors like psilocybin and LSD. However, it produces altered states of consciousness and is being used in similar therapeutic frameworks, which is why it sits within the broader psychedelic medicine conversation.

**How quickly does ketamine work for depression?**
Most people notice significant improvement within 24 to 72 hours of the first infusion. This rapid onset is one of ketamine's most clinically significant advantages over standard antidepressants.

**How long do the effects last?**
A single infusion typically provides relief for one to two weeks. A full course of six infusions can produce sustained improvement for several months. Maintenance infusions monthly or quarterly are common for people who respond well.

**Is Spravato covered by Medicare in Australia?**
Spravato was listed on the PBS in May 2025, which significantly reduces the cost for eligible patients with treatment-resistant depression. Consult your psychiatrist about whether you meet the PBS eligibility criteria.

**Can I access ketamine if I am already on antidepressants?**
In most cases, yes. Unlike psilocybin, which has significant interactions with SSRIs, ketamine can generally be used alongside existing antidepressant regimens. Your prescribing doctor will review your medications before treatment.
