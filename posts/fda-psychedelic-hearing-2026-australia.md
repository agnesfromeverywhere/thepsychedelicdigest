**On September 14, 2026, the United States Food and Drug Administration held its first ever public hearing on the potential therapeutic use of psychedelic drugs. The testimony was overwhelmingly positive. The implications are global.**

For Australians, this matters more than it might seem. Australia moved first on psychedelic therapy regulation in 2023. The US is now catching up fast, and what happens at the FDA in the next six to twelve months will shape how the TGA and Australian providers respond.

Here is what happened and what it means.

## What Actually Happened at the FDA Hearing

The September 14 hearing brought together drug developers, clinicians, researchers, veterans, advocates, and patient voices at the FDA's headquarters. The agenda centred on the potential supervised therapeutic use of psychedelic drugs, specifically psilocybin and a methylone analogue of MDMA.

The testimony was described by observers as overwhelmingly positive. Speakers addressed how psychedelics would actually be administered in the real world, the challenge of long session times (six to ten hours of chair time per dosing experience), cost, access, and the role of the therapeutic container in outcomes.

The FDA is accepting written public comments through October 5, 2026, at Docket No. FDA-2026-N-7542. If you want to make your voice heard, that deadline is this Sunday.

## 5 Moments That Mattered

**1. The FDA finalised its first ever psychedelic clinical trial guidance in July 2026.**

Before the hearing, the FDA published formal guidance for clinical trials involving psychedelic drugs, covering trial design, participant safety, abuse potential assessment, and data collection standards. This is the document drug developers have been waiting years for. It reduces regulatory uncertainty and clears the path for late-stage submissions.

**2. Compass Pathways is targeting a Q4 2026 NDA submission for psilocybin.**

Compass Pathways delivered two positive Phase 3 results in treatment-resistant depression with its synthetic psilocybin formulation COMP360. It is now advancing toward an FDA New Drug Application, targeting final submission in Q4 2026, with commercial launch anticipated in the first half of 2027 subject to approval and DEA rescheduling. This would be the first FDA-approved psychedelic medicine.

**3. Definium Therapeutics produced positive Phase 3 LSD data.**

Definium's DT120, an oral disintegrating LSD tablet, produced positive Phase 3 results for generalised anxiety disorder. LSD as a regulated medicine is no longer a theoretical conversation.

**4. Trump's executive order is accelerating everything.**

President Trump signed an executive order directing the acceleration of federal psychedelic research, fast-tracked FDA reviews, and expanded patient access. Three psychedelic-focused companies received FDA priority review vouchers in the week following the order. RFK Jr., who oversees the FDA as Health Secretary, has repeatedly stated his support for psychedelic approvals.

**5. The biggest unresolved question is still access and administration.**

The most consistent theme at the hearing was not whether psychedelics work. It was how they will actually be delivered once approved. Six to ten hours of supervised chair time per session, two trained therapists, and significant facility requirements make the current model expensive and difficult to scale. This is the same problem Australia is already living with.

## What This Means for Australians

Australia approved psilocybin and MDMA therapy in 2023. The US is now expected to follow with psilocybin approval in 2027. This creates an interesting dynamic.

Australian providers currently operate in a small, high-cost market with no international regulatory comparator to lean on. Once the FDA approves psilocybin, the global evidence base expands dramatically, the commercial infrastructure scales, and the case for Medicare and private health insurance reimbursement in Australia becomes significantly stronger.

There is also a more immediate effect. TGA policy tends to track FDA and EMA approvals over time. A US approval of a specific psilocybin formulation does not automatically change Australia's Authorised Prescriber framework, but it shifts the regulatory conversation. Expanded indications beyond treatment-resistant depression become easier to argue. Nurse practitioner prescribing rights become easier to advocate for.

The US catching up is good for Australian patients. Not immediately. But within a two to three year window, the ripple effects will be felt here.
