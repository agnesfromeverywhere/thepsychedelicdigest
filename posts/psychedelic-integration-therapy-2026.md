There is a gap in almost every conversation about psychedelic therapy. People talk about the experience itself: the visions, the insights, the emotional releases. What gets far less attention is what happens after. Integration therapy is the process of making meaning from a psychedelic experience and translating it into lasting change. Without it, even the most profound journey can fade like a dream by Tuesday morning.

## What Is Psychedelic Integration Therapy?

Integration therapy is a specialised form of psychotherapy designed to help people process, understand, and apply the insights from psychedelic experiences. It is not the same as the therapy that happens during a dosing session. It is what comes after.

**The core assumption of integration therapy is that the experience is not the treatment. The work of integration is the treatment.** A psilocybin session might open a door, but integration is the process of walking through it and making changes on the other side.

Integration sessions typically involve talking through the content of the experience, working with the emotions and imagery that arose, connecting the insights to current life patterns, and developing concrete changes in behaviour, relationship, or perspective.

## Why Is Integration So Important?

Research consistently shows that the quality of the integration process is one of the strongest predictors of long-term outcomes from psychedelic-assisted therapy.

In MAPS' Phase 3 trials of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD, the three preparatory sessions and three integration sessions are considered clinically essential, not optional extras. The MDMA session is one component of a structured therapeutic process.

**A [2021 study](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphar.2021.626604/full) published in Frontiers in Pharmacology found that integration support significantly enhanced the durability of therapeutic benefits from psilocybin-assisted therapy.** Participants with high-quality integration support showed greater and more sustained reductions in depression compared to those with minimal follow-up.

The reason is neurobiological as well as psychological. Psilocybin induces a period of heightened neuroplasticity, during which the brain is more capable of forming new patterns. Integration therapy is the process of actively shaping what those new patterns become.

## What Happens in an Integration Session?

Every integration therapist works differently, but common elements include reviewing the content of the journey in detail, identifying recurring themes or symbols, somatic work to process physical and emotional residue, meaning-making around difficult or confronting material, and developing concrete practices or commitments for daily life.

Integration is not crisis management. It is not trying to explain away what happened. It is a collaborative process of understanding what the experience revealed and deciding what to do with that revelation.

Some people need one or two integration sessions. Others benefit from ongoing support over months. The right amount depends on the depth of the experience, the person's existing capacity for self-reflection, and what changes they are trying to make.

## Who Offers Integration Therapy in Australia?

Australia has a small but growing community of trained integration therapists. Some work within clinical settings offering psilocybin or MDMA through the TGA Authorised Prescriber scheme. Others work independently, supporting people who have had experiences through ceremonies, retreats, or informal contexts.

Organisations including [Inner Focus Therapy](https://www.innerfocustherapy.com.au/), Creating Pathways, and Sydney Integration Therapy offer integration support. Not all integration therapists are prescribers, and they do not need to be. Their role is support, not clinical administration of substances.

## What Is the Difference Between Integration Therapy and Standard Psychotherapy?

Standard psychotherapy is not designed for the specific content that can arise in psychedelic experiences. Themes of ego dissolution, mystical states, confrontation with mortality, and non-ordinary perception require a therapist who understands these experiences and does not pathologise them.

Integration therapists are trained to hold space for the full range of psychedelic experience without reducing it to symptoms or dismissing it as hallucination. The therapeutic relationship in integration work is often characterised by deeper-than-usual trust and vulnerability, which makes the quality of the therapist's training and presence particularly important.

## FAQ

**Can I do integration therapy without having had a clinical psilocybin session?**
Yes. Integration therapy is relevant for any psychedelic experience, including informal or ceremonial ones. Many people seek integration support after retreats, ceremonies, or personal experiences.

**How long does integration therapy last?**
It varies. Most practitioners recommend a minimum of three sessions after any significant psychedelic experience. Ongoing integration work over months is common for people making deep life changes.

**What if the integration brings up difficult material?**
This is expected and normal. A skilled integration therapist is trained to support the processing of challenging emotional content. If difficult material arises, that is often a sign the integration process is working.

**Do I need a referral for integration therapy in Australia?**
No. Integration therapists can be accessed directly without a referral. They are not prescribers and do not require TGA authorisation.

**Is integration therapy covered by Medicare in Australia?**
Standard Medicare rebates for psychology or psychiatry sessions may apply depending on the therapist's qualifications and your mental health care plan. Integration therapy specific to psychedelic experiences is not a separately rebated item.
