Something happened this morning that the mental health world has been waiting years to see.

Definium Therapeutics announced positive topline results from Voyage, its Phase 3 study of DT120, an orally disintegrating tablet formulation of lysergide, in adults with generalised anxiety disorder. The study hit its primary endpoint with statistical significance and a large effect size. It swept every key secondary endpoint. And it did so just eight weeks after the company announced positive Phase 3 results from the Emerge study in major depressive disorder.

Two Phase 3 wins. Two different psychiatric conditions. Eight weeks apart. From the same compound.

This is not a routine clinical update. This is the kind of data readout that reshapes a sector.

## What Did the Voyage Study Actually Show?

Voyage enrolled 214 participants aged 18 to 74 across 35 US sites, all with DSM-5 confirmed generalised anxiety disorder and a baseline Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale score of at least 20. Participants were randomised one to one to a single 100 microgram dose of DT120 ODT or placebo, followed by a 12-week double-blind period and a 40-week open-label extension in which participants could receive up to four additional doses based on symptom severity.

**The primary endpoint was change in HAM-A total score from baseline to week 12.**

DT120 produced a least-squares mean change of -11.6 points from baseline. Placebo produced -6.2 points. The placebo-adjusted difference was -5.4 points, with a p-value below 0.0001 and a Cohen's d effect size of 0.81.

A Cohen's d of 0.81 is considered a large effect size by conventional clinical standards. For context, most approved pharmacological treatments for GAD produce effect sizes in the small to medium range. **DT120 produced a large effect size from a single dose over 12 weeks.**

The secondary endpoints told an equally compelling story. <cite index="3-1">43% of DT120-treated patients achieved a 50% or greater improvement in HAM-A score at week 12, compared to 16% on placebo.</cite> Critically, <cite index="5-1">improvements emerged by Day 2 and were maintained across all assessed timepoints through week 12.</cite> The effect was rapid, meaningful, and durable over the study period.

Rob Barrow, CEO of Definium Therapeutics, described the results in terms that are unusual for clinical press releases in their directness. <cite index="5-1">"The unprecedented efficacy demonstrated in Voyage should raise the bar for what patients and clinicians expect from GAD treatments."</cite>

## Why Generalised Anxiety Disorder Matters

Generalised anxiety disorder is one of the most prevalent and most undertreated psychiatric conditions in the world. <cite index="4-1">Approximately 26 million US adults are affected by GAD.</cite> It is characterised by persistent, excessive worry that is difficult to control and is accompanied by physical symptoms including fatigue, muscle tension, concentration difficulties, and sleep disruption.

<cite index="3-1">Despite the significant personal and societal burden of GAD, there has been little innovation in its treatment in the past several decades, with the last new drug approval occurring in 2007.</cite>

Read that again. 2007. Nearly two decades without a meaningful new treatment option for a condition affecting tens of millions of people. The existing treatments, primarily SSRIs, SNRIs, and buspirone, work for some people and fail for many others. Side effect profiles are often significant. Withdrawal is difficult. The gap between what patients need and what is available has been growing for years.

DT120 is proposing a fundamentally different approach. Not a daily pill modulating neurotransmitter levels. A single dose, administered in a supervised clinical setting, producing clinically significant reductions in anxiety that emerge within two days and persist for at least 12 weeks.

## The Commercial Picture

<cite index="4-1">Definium estimates a potential $2 billion revenue opportunity per 1% penetration across GAD and MDD based on internal analyses.</cite> That is a substantial commercial target and the clinical data now supports it across two Phase 3 studies in two different major indications.

The session length question is real and worth understanding clearly. Definium guides that it expects predictable 5 to 8 hour sessions. In the Voyage GAD population, the average session clearance time was 6.4 hours, slightly higher than the 5.8 hour average seen in the MDD Emerge study. 56% of Voyage participants cleared the end-of-session checklist at hour 6, with 8% yet to complete it at hour 8.

This is a commercialisation challenge that Definium and every other company developing long-acting psychedelic medicines shares. A 6 to 8 hour session requires specialised clinical infrastructure, trained staff, appropriate spaces, and a payor framework that can account for a fundamentally different treatment delivery model from anything currently standard in psychiatry.

However, there is a structural tailwind here that is easy to miss. If COMPASS Pathways and other companies with psilocybin programs that also require extended sessions go to market first, they will do a significant amount of the infrastructure-building work. Definium may benefit from following them into a clinical ecosystem that has already been partially built and partially reimbursed.

## What Comes Next

<cite index="2-1">Topline results from the Phase 3 Panorama study in GAD, which features both a 50 microgram control arm and an inert placebo, are expected in late Q3 2026.</cite> This second GAD study will add important data about dose response and provide an active comparator arm that Voyage did not include.

A second Phase 3 study in MDD is also in progress. A PTSD study is planned for 2027 initiation. The pipeline is expanding across multiple major psychiatric indications simultaneously.

The most consequential open question is whether Definium will wait for all of this data before filing a New Drug Application with the FDA, or whether the two positive Phase 3 readouts in GAD alone are sufficient to support an accelerated submission pathway. Management has signalled confidence in the possibility of filing as early as the first half of 2027 for the GAD indication.

If that timeline holds and the FDA review proceeds efficiently, DT120 could be an approved medicine before the end of the decade. Given the 19-year gap in new GAD approvals, the clinical need, and the effect size this data has produced, the case for prioritised review is not difficult to make.

## FAQ

**What is DT120 and how does it work?**
DT120 is an orally disintegrating tablet formulation of lysergide, the active compound in LSD, developed by Definium Therapeutics. At the 100 microgram dose used in clinical trials it produces a supervised psychedelic experience lasting 5 to 8 hours in a clinical setting. It works through serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonism, producing changes in brain connectivity and neuroplasticity that appear to produce sustained reductions in anxiety and depression symptoms from a single administration.

**What is generalised anxiety disorder?**
GAD is a psychiatric condition characterised by persistent, excessive, and difficult-to-control worry about a range of topics, accompanied by physical symptoms including fatigue, muscle tension, sleep disruption, and difficulty concentrating. It affects approximately 26 million US adults and has had no new drug approval since 2007.

**What did the Voyage Phase 3 study find?**
Voyage found that a single 100 microgram dose of DT120 ODT produced a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in HAM-A anxiety scores at week 12 compared to placebo. The placebo-adjusted difference was 5.4 points with a p-value below 0.0001 and a Cohen's d effect size of 0.81. 43% of DT120 patients achieved 50% or greater improvement compared to 16% on placebo. Effects emerged by Day 2 and were maintained through week 12.

**When could DT120 be approved?**
Definium has indicated the possibility of filing an NDA for the GAD indication in the first half of 2027, following the second GAD Phase 3 readout from Panorama expected in late Q3 2026. FDA approval could potentially follow by 2028 if the review proceeds on a standard timeline.

**What does this mean for psychedelic medicine investors?**
Two positive Phase 3 results in two major psychiatric indications from the same compound represents a significant de-risking event for the compound and adds credibility to the broader psychedelic medicine investment thesis. DFTX shares traded up 10% at market open following the announcement.
