The psychedelic clinical trials landscape has never been more active. As of mid-2026, over 100 studies are running worldwide, spanning psilocybin, MDMA, LSD, DMT, and ketamine. Several are reading out this year and could permanently change how we treat mental illness.

## Which Psilocybin Trial Is Most Important Right Now?

PSILODEP-2, run by Monash University, is the most significant psilocybin trial currently active in the Southern Hemisphere. It is a phase 2 randomised controlled trial of psilocybin-assisted therapy for treatment-resistant depression, with 160 participants across multiple sites. **Treatment-resistant depression affects approximately 100 million people worldwide**, and one-third of all depression patients fail to respond to existing medications. Results are expected in Q1 2027.

At the same time, COMPASS Pathways' COMP360 psilocybin program is the most advanced commercial psilocybin asset globally, with phase 2b data already published and phase 3 now underway. The FDA granted COMP360 a national priority review voucher in 2026, meaning approval could come as early as late 2026 or 2027.

## What Is the Status of MDMA for PTSD?

MAPS-2 is the confirmatory phase 3 trial of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD, building on the landmark results of the first phase 3 study. **In MAPS' first phase 3 trial, 67% of participants no longer qualified for a PTSD diagnosis after three MDMA sessions**, compared to 32% in the placebo group. MAPS-2 is fully enrolled, with results expected in late 2026 or early 2027. A positive readout would put MDMA on track for FDA approval by 2028.

## What Other Conditions Are Being Studied?

The scope of psychedelic research in 2026 extends well beyond depression and PTSD. Active trials are studying:

- **Anorexia nervosa** at the University of Melbourne, one of the few trials globally targeting eating disorders
- **Alcohol use disorder** through MindMed's phase 2b LSD trial, the largest LSD study in 50 years with 200 participants
- **OCD** through a psilocybin phase 2 study showing early promise
- **End-of-life anxiety** through multiple palliative care partnerships
- **Alzheimer's disease** through an upcoming Johns Hopkins phase 2 psilocybin study
- **Smoking cessation** through psilocybin trials at multiple institutions

## What Does This Mean for Australia?

Australia's 2023 TGA rescheduling of psilocybin and MDMA removed key bureaucratic barriers for local researchers, and the scientific community moved fast. Australia now has over 25 active psychedelic trials, concentrated in Sydney and Melbourne but expanding nationally. The Australian National University is running a real-world outcomes registry collecting data from every patient treated under the Authorised Prescriber scheme, which will be critical evidence for future PBS listing.

## How Do I Find a Psychedelic Trial Near Me?

The two main registries are [ANZCTR](https://www.anzctr.org.au/) for Australia and New Zealand, and [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/) for global trials. Search for "psilocybin", "MDMA", or your specific condition plus Australia. Many trials also recruit through university psychiatry departments before listing publicly.

**What is the most advanced psilocybin trial?**

**When will MDMA therapy be approved?**
If MAPS-2 replicates the results of the first phase 3 trial, MDMA could be approved for PTSD treatment as early as 2028.

**Do participants get paid for joining psychedelic trials?**
Most Australian trials do not pay participants but may reimburse travel. The primary benefit is receiving investigational treatment at no cost.

**Can I join a trial if I am on antidepressants?**
Many trials exclude participants on SSRIs due to receptor interactions. Check each trial's eligibility criteria carefully before applying.
